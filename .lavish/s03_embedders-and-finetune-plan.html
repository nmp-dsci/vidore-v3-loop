The short answer. A text embedder is the cheaper and safer model to fine-tune. It re-encodes all 2,942 pages in minutes, where the visual index took 11 hours. So both questions and pages can learn, and every cross-validation fold gets a fresh index. The small visual model runs on the Mac for $0. Modal earns its place only for the cross-validation sweep: 20 short runs in parallel for about $3–5. The open question is whether 155 questions can lift text from about 50 to the point where fusing it with EVIE pays.

What changed from the first draft. Part A indexes one small visual model on the Mac instead of three. Part B trains a text embedder instead of EVIE. The Modal estimate drops from ≈ $25–45 to ≈ $3–8. The 8–9B visual models, Nemotron 3.5 and the visual fine-tuning ladder are parked.

Parked: pplx-embed-v2-late-9b (73.9), EVIE-8B (73.7) and the Nemotron visual models. They all need Modal to index, and none is more than 1.6 points above EVIE on the board.

The catch: test and train share pages

115 of the 154 test questions share at least one gold page with a train question. 39 share none. Today EVIE scores 66.7 on the 115 and 84.0 on the 39.

Training both sides teaches the model the 471 train gold pages, 218 of which are also test gold pages. A gain only on the 115 means memorised pages, not better retrieval, so every fine-tuned test run reports both slices.

How small a gain can the test show? Per-question NDCG@10 has a standard deviation of 28.0 points, so the mean over 154 questions has an SE of 2.25. Plan on +2–3 points as the smallest real gain, checked with a paired bootstrap.

A model trained on finance_en questions is no longer zero-shot. Its row goes on the Leaderboard tab marked "adapted on train", never ranked among the board's rows.

How a trained model gets scored

Choose on train onlyUse 5-fold cross-validation by question, stratified by query type with seed 300. Each fold trains on 124 questions, re-encodes all pages and scores its held-out 31.
Retrain on all 155Retrain the chosen setting on all 155 train questions. The checkpoint (≈ 1.2 GB) goes to data/cache/models/text/, and the run's params and metrics go to MLflow.
Test onceThe config fingerprint gains text_model, the checkpoint hash and the three weights, so the test guard treats it as a new config: one --final run each. That run reports the full score plus the "shared gold" (115) and "unseen gold" (39) slices.
Answers lastRun answers only for a retrieval winner. They reuse the answer cache wherever the top 5 pages are unchanged.

My expectation, not a measurement: zero-shot Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B lands near BM25S, since its 8B sibling scores 54.8. Fine-tuning should add several points on train cross-validation. Whether fusion then beats EVIE alone by the 2–3 points the test can show is the open question, and B0–B2 settle it for under $5. Visual fine-tuning, the first draft's ladder, is parked. If text training shows the 155 questions carry signal, the recipe can move to pplx-0.6b's question side.

6 · where vLLM fits

This plan doesn't need vLLM

What vLLM would add

  • Fast GPU serving of embedding models: the Qwen3-Embedding card shows vLLM usage, and vLLM pools tokens for ColQwen3 and ColQwen3.5
  • No training, and nothing on a Mac without CUDA
  • At 2,942 pages, encodes already take minutes with sentence-transformers

Recommendation

Use sentence-transformers everywhere: one code path for the Mac, Modal and training. The branch name says vLLM, but it becomes worth it only if we later serve questions from a GPU.
