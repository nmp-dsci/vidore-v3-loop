Why Haiku is stricter

All 40 of its disagreements turn Opus's Correct into Partially Correct, and each explanation points to extra detail. The paper's prompt does say "substantial extra information" earns Partially Correct, so Haiku reads it literally. An example: the question "Citigroup share repurchases 2024" has the reference "$2.5 billion". The answer gave $2.5 billion plus share counts and the 2025 programme. Opus and Sonnet called it Correct; Haiku didn't.

Two caveats

Sonnet's agreement depends a little on whose answers it judges. On Haiku's S2 answers it matched Opus on 94.2%, 2.6 points stricter, mostly on human-page answers. The plan's Opus spot-check of 50 agent answers at S5 covers this.

With no labels from you yet, "matches Opus" is the only test. rejudge.rescore(<check>) re-scores every check once your labels exist.

How close it is

The rule was written for the agent at S5. Applied to the S2 pipeline, Haiku lands 0.4 points under the line with the Sonnet judge and 0.2 over it with Opus, so the top-5 figure alone is a coin flip. The human-page result isn't: −9.0 points, p = 0.003. That's reading ability, which no amount of searching fixes.

So the agent starts on Sonnet 5.5

R1 (a Haiku agent) is skipped and S5 opens at R2: Sonnet 5.5 · high answers, Sonnet 5.5 · high judges. If Sonnet also lands under 61.7% on train, we stop and review, as you decided. Haiku stays the default for single answers in the viewer, where cost matters more than a few points.
